What the two artifacts are

One is the reference build. One is the standard it is the reference for.

Artifact What it is Size Lines
Ward Flow Command

Ward Flow Design System
e7f7b6f2		 The third-edition standard every Ward Flow mockup is built to: ten rules, tokens, type, material, components rendered live from Command's own stylesheet, behaviour, wording, an accessibility floor, a definition of done, a recipe, the migration plan for eighteen mockups and an index of twelve screens. 649 KB 16,162

The lineage matters when reading either one. The first edition was Platinum Raised Cool and supplied the identity: cool platinum neutrals, deep slate brand, brass secondary, three faces, a fall of light. The second was the Live edition and supplied the content, behaviour and rigour: derived figures, route words, the diagram fill rule, the three-state appearance control, the type floor, the contrast table and the definition of done. The third edition is the second's substance in the first's material, and where the two disagreed the stricter rule won.

Both are design intent, not app code. The application resolves colour by role through its own token layer, so a hex from either artifact must never be pasted into src/. Ward Flow does exist as real code in this repository, under src/components/ward-management/ and src/app/mockups/ward-flow/, and section 02 records where the mockup's rules and the shipped engine's rules disagree.

02

Review and findings

Eight findings, worst first. Two are safety questions for the owner, not defects to fix quietly.

Judged as a design standard, this is unusually strong work. The rules are written as things a script can check rather than as taste, the contrast figures were computed and printed rather than eyeballed, and the hardest clinical rule in the build, the split between what a recorded reason can get past and what it cannot, is written as a fail-closed list so a gate nobody has classified cannot become overridable by omission. The findings below are about the seams: between the standard and its own reference build, and between the mockup and the engine that ships.

ContradictionThe mockup and the shipped engine disagree about a prior decline F1

Command treats prior_decline as absolute. Its sentence is fixed: "A recorded reason does not undo a ward's decline." The shipped reducer deliberately does the opposite. It leaves prior_decline out of both the world-fact group and SUITABILITY_GATES, on the reasoning that a ward which declined ninety minutes ago because it had no bed is the commonest thing in bed management, beds free up, and none of the five approved override reasons names a mismatch that exists. The comment records that enforcing it broke 23 tests across 7 files.

Why it matters: a coordinator taught by the prototype believes a declined ward is closed to that patient. The engine believes re-approaching it is routine and needs no reason at all. That is not a styling difference, it is two different accounts of what the coordinator may do next. Fix: one of the two has to move, and the reducer already records the question as open for you. Decide it, then write the decision into section 8.4 of the standard so the next mockup inherits it.

Safety questionMental Health Act authorisation is overridable in both F2

The standard's own test is clean: "A judgement about the patient is overridable by a named coordinator with a recorded reason. A fact about the world is not." Command classifies authorisation as overridable, and so does the engine, where it sits in SUITABILITY_GATES alongside cohort and security as a clinical judgement.

Why it matters: whether a ward is an authorised hospital under the Mental Health Act 2014 (WA) is a fact about the ward's legal status, not a judgement about the patient. A recorded reason cannot make the detention of an involuntary patient at an unauthorised ward lawful, in the same way it cannot create a bed. By the standard's own test the gate belongs beside allocatable_bed. The same reading applies to security, where whether a locked bed exists is also a fact rather than a judgement. Fix: this is your ruling to make, not mine, and it may already have been made somewhere I have not read. Either move it, or write the reasoning for leaving it where it is into section 8.4, because at the moment the classification and the stated test do not agree and nothing on the page explains why.

DivergenceTen gates in the mockup, twelve in the engine F3

Command emits allocatable_bed, capability, security, sex_mix, acuity, specialling, authorisation, catchment, prior_decline and freshness. The engine's ELIGIBILITY_GATES adds age, forensic and legal_status, splits sex_designation out from sex_mix, and carries no acuity or catchment gate at all.

Why it matters: the standard governs colour, type and wording in detail and says nothing about the rule model, so there is no mechanism keeping the two lists together. They have already drifted. Fix: a short subsection in section 8 naming the engine as the source of truth for gates, and a line in the definition of done requiring a mockup's gate list to be a subset of ELIGIBILITY_GATES.

DefectThe standard's ward and site counts are not true of its own reference build F4

Section 8.3 states there are twenty-three wards across seventeen sites, and that a page reads the collection rather than carrying a hard-coded number. Command carries sixteen wards across nine sites, and no WA Country ward at all, although WACHS is one of the services and has a hue of its own.

Why it matters: section 6 of the standard renders its components from Command, so the standard is asserting a figure its own reference build contradicts. Anyone building the next mockup will take twenty-three from the prose and find sixteen in the data. Fix: either extend Command's UNITS to the full network, or restate 8.3 as the network's real size with a note that Command carries a subset and says so. The WACHS absence is already handled well by the "WA Country states its absences" rule, so only the counts need settling.

GapSex and gender are one binary field, and the reconciler enforces the binary F5

A movement carries sex: "Male" | "Female". A ward carries sexDesignation and sexMix: { Female, Male }. That one field drives bed matching, and the page's own reconciliation check fails unless sexMix.Female + sexMix.Male equals occupied. A patient who is intersex or non-binary cannot be represented in the data model at all, and there is no rule about it anywhere in the standard.

Why it matters: this is a real clinical and ethical exposure rather than a modelling nicety, and it is already open in this repository as a P1 (#BAY1TY). Bed allocation, ward designation and a person's gender identity are three different facts and the model has one field for all three. Fix: out of scope for a styling pass, but the standard should say so explicitly, name who owns the question, and stop new mockups copying the binary field forward in the meantime.

GapNothing anywhere addresses Aboriginal cultural safety F6

Neither artifact contains a rule, a field, a gate or a note on Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander cultural safety. For a statewide WA mental health bed placement tool covering WACHS, that absence is conspicuous. It is open in this repository as a P1 (#G4YPNE), recorded as work this team cannot do alone.

Fix: the standard is the right place to record that the gap is known, deliberate and owned elsewhere, rather than leaving each mockup to notice it independently. A page that says nothing reads as a page that decided nothing.

UnstatedThe shortlist orders wards, and the standard never admits it F7

shortlistFor() sorts candidate wards by availability class and then by ready beds descending. That is an ordering, and an ordering at the top of a screen reads as a recommendation. The standard is scrupulous that no verdict is ever drawn about a person, and silent about whether the ward ordering is a suggestion. The same question is open in this repository as #6DT5K7, recorded there as a rule that may be reversed and is written down nowhere.

Fix: one sentence in section 8.4 settling it. Either the order is a suggestion and the page says so plainly, or it is not and the page says what the order actually means, which on the evidence is eligible first, then most ready beds. Both are defensible. Neither being written down is not.

PracticalBoth artifacts are now too large to be read whole F8

376 KB and 649 KB, of which the great majority is the stylesheet, the engine and the live component demos. The stylesheet appears in full in both, twice in the design system. Reading either one costs a large share of a session's context before any work begins.

Fix: this file. Keep the two artifacts as the built truth and hand this brief to a model instead.

Worth keepingWhat the build gets right, and should not be traded away F9

The absolute-gate list is written as the absolute side, so an unclassified gate fails closed rather than becoming overridable by omission, and a check at the foot of the file fails if a gate named there has no sentence of its own. reconcile() refuses to let a movement be pointed at a ward with an unmet absolute gate, so the invented data cannot quietly contradict the rules. Refusals are sentences rather than empty lists. Absence is stated everywhere, zero reads none, and direction is a word rather than an arrow, which survives a copy into a progress note. The recorded history of two earlier wordings that were wrong in opposite directions is kept in the source so neither returns by accident. That last habit is worth more than any of the visual rules.

03

The ten rules

In the order they matter. Everything else in the standard is a consequence of one of them.

1

State is a word first, and a colour second.

A colour, bar or tint only repeats a word already on the screen, so a reader with no colour perception loses nothing and neither does a greyscale printout. Red means breach, amber means look here, green means clear. The brand slate and the brass never carry a status.

2 **Figures are Geist Mono and tabular.**

Every identifier, wait, count, time and code, so anything compared down a column lines up and a digit never jitters. Zero reads none: a nought is a measurement and none is a state.

3

Absence is stated, never blank.

"Not tracked here" and "No destination yet" are the models. An empty panel says why it is empty and what the emptiness means. A count of zero shows in italic without a pill rather than hiding. A panel that is merely empty reads as a bug.

4

Nothing below 12px, and seven steps in the scale.

Uppercase labels carry 0.08 to 0.12em of tracking, headings a little negative tracking. Only loaded weights are asked for. No small capitals. The wordmark is t-5 at 600, not a size of its own.

5

One elevation step, and only one.

Light falls from the top. A panel is lifted by an alpha hairline heavier along its bottom edge, and one low brand-tinted shadow. No highlight along its top, and no coloured bar along any edge of a row, a candidate or a card, since 9 September 2026. Nothing inside a panel is lifted again. Radii step inward: 10px panels, 9px strips, 6px controls, a pill for chips. Hairlines are solid, never dashed. Sideways overflow shows a soft shade and a sentence, never a frame. A list that continues past its window fades into that edge, measured on every render, scroll and resize.

6

Colour has four jobs and they never share a hue.

Slate accent means brand and interactive. Brass means "you are here" and is a bar, never a fill: the active nav item, the live tab, the current stage, the two letters beside the wordmark, nothing else. Green, amber and red mean status only. The health services have hues of their own, except East Metropolitan, which keeps the brand slate as a recorded exception because no fifth hue stays apart from plum, teal and rust. On a map only an eligible or recorded ward carries a fill and every other verdict is an outline and a word.

7

Every text pairing is measured in both themes at a floor of 4.5:1.

Sixty-nine pairs, computed by the build script rather than typed, and recomputed by the page from its own tokens on every load. A pair that falls short is printed, marked and reported, never hidden.

8

Both themes are tokens only.

The bare root is the complete light palette. Dark is redefined under the machine's preference, guarded so an explicit light choice wins, and again under the page's own control so that wins the other way. For print every token takes its light value on all three roots. Never a colour whose only definition sits inside a theme block, never a hex in a component, never pure black or pure white as a surface or an ink.

9

Every figure is invented and the page says so twice.

Once in the bar, where the prototype mark carries the disclaimer as its tooltip, and once in the rail foot beside the reconciliation line. Every count on the rail, the bar and in the drawers is derived from the data on the page, never typed.

10

Optical, not arithmetic.

A tracked uppercase label ending a line carries its trailing tracking as a negative margin. An inner corner is one pixel tighter than its panel. Every control draws rest, hover, pressed and focus, a disabled control says why and does not light on hover, and nothing brightens on hover. Selection never hides status. Every change of subject is spoken to a screen reader as a sentence.

06

Material and layout

One shadow, one fall of light, radii that step inward.

  • The canvas is a fixed vertical gradient from --ground-hi through --ground to --ground-2, so light falls from the top of the window. A 3px --stripe sits fixed at the top edge above everything including the drawers, with pointer events off.
  • A panel is --surface, an alpha hairline all round with --line-strong along the bottom, radius 10px, and one shadow: 0 1px 1px plus a long low 0 14px 30px -22px tinted with the brand, and no highlight on top. Nothing inside a panel gets a second shadow.
  • Header strips, tab bars and diagram feet sit on --surface-2 and take the 9px inner radius where they meet a panel corner.
  • The shell is a skip link, one rail at 236px in two states, one row of bar with search, service selector, three drawers and one primary action, one polite live region, and a scrolling column of panels. Command's body is a three-column grid: 15rem, flexible, 25rem.
  • Panel bodies are one of five things and those five cover every screen so far: a list of rows, a strip of cards, a diagram with a foot and a legend, tabs, or sections with headings.
  • Widths proved: the bar has no overflow at 1920, 1600, 1440, 1280 and 1100, and search never falls below 10rem. The rail has no sideways overflow in either state and no state line truncates at 236px. Below 1000px the rail renders open as a wrapping row and its control hides.

08

Behaviour

Six states, measured affordances, and one sentence per change of subject.

State Looks like Rule
Rest Surface, hairline, ink-soft text Every control draws it explicitly
Hover The well, ink-soft border on a control, thicker outline on the map Never recolours a status, never lifts, never brightens, never on a disabled control
Pressed The same as hover, so a click has a felt end point Distinct from selected
Focus 2px accent outline, out on controls, in on rows and lists, 2.5px ink on a map node focus-visible only. Everything focusable has a 12px scroll margin
Selected Accent-soft fill, accent-ink text, a brass bar under a tab, a slate ring on a card Never hides status. Announced
Chosen Good-soft fill on the candidate that is now the destination Distinct from showing. A candidate can be showing without being chosen
Disabled Half opacity, not-allowed cursor, no shadow, nothing changes on hover Uses aria-disabled so it stays focusable and the reason stays reachable

Measured, never declared

List fades come from scrollHeight, clientHeight and scrollTop on every render, scroll and resize. Sideways overflow comes from scrollWidth against clientWidth, and the shade and the sentence appear and disappear together. Every count in the rail, the bar, the drawers, the tab strip and every panel header is derived from the data, and the reconciliation line compares them.

Keyboard

  • Tab reaches the skip link first, then the rail, the bar, then each panel in reading order.
  • Tab lists take arrow keys with a roving tabindex, Home and End to the ends. Rows, cards and map nodes are buttons and take Enter and Space.
  • / focuses the search and selects its text. [ flips the rail and remembers it. Neither fires inside a field or while a modifier is held.
  • Escape clears exactly one thing per press, from the top: an open pop out, the search results, the search text, the ward selection, the referral subject, the department filter, then the task filter. Escape never clears the service: a further press says so and names it. Each step is announced and the shell owns the order.
  • Every scrolling region is itself focusable so its contents can be scrolled from the keyboard.

Announcements

One sentence to the live region per change of subject, never a fragment and never a colour name. A repeated sentence gets a zero width space so it is read again. Models: "WF-014 selected. Shortlist shows three candidates." "Showing 4 of 23 waiting, from Joondalup Health Campus ED." "Service set to South Metropolitan. Showing 7 of 23." "The rail, closed."

Themes, motion, print, forced colours

Appearance has three states. Auto stamps nothing and follows the machine; light and dark stamp data-theme and are remembered under a key named for the page. Motion is a 120ms transition on colour, background, border, a chevron's turn and a node's outline weight, and nothing else; nothing moves on load; reduced motion removes all of it. Print is a record: the rail, stripe and controls go, scrolling regions open out, tabs and disclosures open, panels lose shadow and radius, every token takes its light value and status colours print exact. Forced colours keep every boundary as CanvasText on Canvas.

Pop outs and drawers

One open at a time. Each is a details.menu. Opening one closes the others and the search results. A click outside or on the backdrop closes it, Escape closes it and returns focus to its summary, the first focusable thing inside takes focus on open, the panel is a dialog with aria-modal, everything outside it is inert and Tab wraps inside it. Opening is announced with the drawer's count in the sentence; closing announces "Closed."

09

Wording and data honesty

The Board language's rules, kept. This is the section that carries the clinical safety weight.

About persons

  • No verdict about a person is ever drawn. Verdicts are about wards, beds, checks and movements.
  • A referral's written history is never given a green state, because a history is not an outcome.
  • Search refuses a risk or acuity score, a best match, and closed or arrived movements, and says so as a sentence in a refused-action row rather than returning nothing.
  • Nobody's move is not nobody's problem: a movement with no owner still carries its severity.
  • A judgement about the patient is overridable by a named coordinator with a recorded reason. A fact about the world is not. A check nobody has classified is not overridable.

Copy style

  • Sentence case everywhere. Uppercase only as a tracked label at t-0 or t-1. Australian spelling.
  • No semicolons, no dashes as punctuation, no arrows, no symbol a digital medical record would not carry. A middle dot separates facts on one line and never joins two sentences. Where the line is prose, a comma serves.
  • Direction is a word: from and to, up and down, in and out.
  • Times as 10:42 with AWST beside the date. Dates as Sat 15 Aug in a tile, in full when read aloud.
  • A wait reads 25h 10m, in the compact form, everywhere, from one formatter.
  • A deadline reads "Legal deadline passed 1h 10m ago", or "Deadline in 1h 35m", or "No deadline recorded".
  • A control that is drawn and not wired says so: "Not wired in this prototype."

Invented and real

  • Hospital sites, health services and community team names are real WA names from the repository's tables and may be used. Their phone numbers, addresses and contact details are never invented.
  • Never invent a record number, a UMRN, a phone number, an address, or a person's name that could be mistaken for real. Names are an uncommon given name with a plant, bird or stone family name: Kestrel Wenna, Hawthorn Dermot, Slate Margery.
  • Contact tables use placeholders in the shape of the real thing, ext 01 onward for wards and ext 11 onward for departments, addresses ending in example.invalid, and say so twice.
  • Identifier namespaces are separate on purpose so a click never lands on the wrong object: WF-0xx on Command, WF-1xx on Movement, WF-2xx on Capacity, RF-0xx for referrals.

10

Accessibility floor

Not negotiable, and mostly machine-checked.

  • Text contrast 4.5:1 for every pairing in both themes, computed per pair and printed. Non-text marks at 3:1.
  • No colour is the only carrier of any state. Every state has a word on the screen.
  • Every interactive thing is a button or a link with an accessible name. Icons are aria-hidden with their word beside them.
  • Tab lists use tablist, tab and tabpanel roles with aria-selected, aria-controls and a roving tabindex. Toggles use aria-pressed, disclosures aria-expanded.
  • Focus is visible on every control, inset inside scrolling regions, with a scroll margin so nothing hides under a header.
  • A skip link is the first focusable element, and one polite live region receives every change of subject.
  • A disabled control says why, in its title and a described-by line, and stays focusable through aria-disabled.
  • Every chart has role img, an aria-label stating the numbers and a written caption stating them again.
  • Tap targets are 3rem where the pointer is coarse or the width is a phone's, and are never reduced to 44px to satisfy a generic rule.
  • Nothing below 12px in HTML, and nothing on the map below 10.5px. The page holds at 200% zoom without loss and never scrolls sideways.
  • Both themes read as well as each other. Dark is designed, not inverted.
11

Definition of done

A claim without its output is not a pass.

A mockup is done when every line is true and has been looked at once, in a real browser, in both themes. The proof is the look and the page's own checks, not a claim. The load-bearing lines:

  • The stylesheet is copied verbatim. Screen rules sit below it under a comment naming the screen. No raw hex below the block.
  • Every size is one of the seven steps in HTML, and one of the map's stated sizes in SVG. Nothing below 12px in HTML and nothing below 10.5px on the map.
  • Every colour has one of the four jobs. Brass is a bar and marks only where the reader is. Red appears only on a breach. At most two tiles are amber.
  • Every state has a word, every absence is stated, every zero reads none, every direction is a word.
  • Every contrast pair the page uses appears in the contrast table, or has been computed and added there first.
  • Lists fade and strips shade by measurement. Every legend is a disclosure. Disclosures open for print.
  • Every change of subject is announced. Escape clears the innermost thing. The skip link is first.

New mockup recipe, and the Never list

Build by copying, not by re-deriving. A gap is reported, never invented.

The recipe

  1. Copy the head: title tag first, two preconnects, the one font link, the language and appearance script before the stylesheet.
  2. Copy the three roots verbatim, plus the print block and the reduced-motion block.
  3. Copy the whole stylesheet unchanged. Add only what the screen needs, below it, under a comment naming the screen.
  4. Copy the appearance script and change the page name in the storage key. Then copy the measurement helpers the page uses.
  5. Keep the shell: skip link, one rail in two states, one row of bar, one live region, a scrolling column of panels. Mark the current rail link with aria-current.
  6. Map data to primitives. A thing with a status gets a card or row whose title or verdict word carries it. A count gets a pill. A category gets a tier pill. A sequence gets the stepper. A label and value pair gets the facts list. One primary action per panel at most.
  7. Before adding a colour, name its meaning in one word. If the word is not breach, look here, clear, a health service, the brand or you are here, the colour is wrong.
  8. State every absence, derive every count, then run the harness and work the definition of done line by line.

Never

  • A second shadow inside a panel. A shadow on a button, chip, card or row.
  • A coloured bar along any edge of a row, a candidate or a card, brass included. A highlight along the top of a panel or a control. The owner removed both on 9 September 2026.
  • A brass fill. A status colour on a title, border, tab or a button that is not itself a status action.
  • A hex value in a component. A new grey. A solid grey used as a line.
  • Small capitals. A weight that is not loaded. A size that is not on the scale.
  • A panel without a header strip. A header strip with controls beyond one small disclosure.
  • A layout that hides a count behind an unopened tab.
  • A second navigation bar, a second rail, or a second scope control. One rail, one bar, one stripe, one composer.
  • A selected state that covers a status word. A hover that brightens.
  • An arrow, a dash as punctuation, a symbol a digital record would not carry.
  • A verdict about a person. An invented phone number, address, record number or real-seeming name.

The one question that settles an element: does this tell a coordinator something they would otherwise have to work out? If yes, it earns its place and takes the primitive that already exists for that kind of fact. If no, it comes off the page.

Travel

Travel time is a fact about a ward and a patient, so it lives on Command rather than Capacity: the selected movement supplies the origin, which is the department the person is actually in. Change the selection and every band re-derives. Only the band shows, never the minutes, because a minute-precise figure for a transfer nobody has booked is false precision dressed as a fact. Bands are under 20 minutes, 20 to 40 minutes and 40 minutes or more.

Placement gates

Ten checks on one axis: can a named coordinator place this person here by recording a reason, or can they not.

The split is written down in exactly one place, and it is written as the absolute side, so a gate nobody has classified cannot become overridable by omission. A check at the foot of the file fails if any gate named as absolute lacks its own sentence.

Gate Passes when Class
allocatable_bed ready > 0 Absolute. "No reason typed into a form creates a bed."
specialling movement.specialling <= unit.speciallingFree Absolute. A recorded reason cannot create one-to-one nursing that does not exist
prior_decline This ward has not declined this movement Absolute. "A recorded reason does not undo a ward's decline." See finding F1
capability Ward cohort matches movement cohort Overridable
security Open requirement, or the ward has a locked bed Overridable. See finding F2
sex_mix Designation accepts this sex, and either a same-sex occupant exists or more than one bed is ready Overridable. See finding F5
acuity Not high acuity, or acuityInUse < acuityCeiling Overridable
authorisation Voluntary, or the ward is authorised under the Mental Health Act Overridable. See finding F2
catchment Ward service matches the person's home service Overridable
freshness confirmedMinAgo <= staleAfter Overridable. Matches the engine's owner ruling of 2 Sep 2026

The five verdicts, and the order they sort in

Order Verdict Means
0 eligible Every gate passes. Carries a fill on the map
1 overridable Only judgement gates fail. A named coordinator can place with a recorded reason
2 declined This ward has declined. Absolute
3 no_specialling The one-to-one nursing is not there. Absolute
4 no_bed There is no bed. Absolute

When more than one absolute gate fails, they are reported in a fixed order so the same pair always reads the same way, and the bed comes first because it settles the rest: allocatable_bed, then specialling, then prior_decline.

Two wordings recorded so they never return

The source keeps both earlier versions that were wrong in opposite directions. The first printed "Cannot take this person" beside a live override button: the refusal was not the error, the live button was, and the words were, because they phrased a fact about a ward as a verdict about a person. The second used a major and minor weight axis with every gate overridable, which put specialling on the minor side, so a recorded reason appeared to place a person into one-to-one nursing that does not exist.

Self-checks

The page derives its own reconciliation line and says out loud whether it holds.

The rail foot shows a green dot and a sentence when the figures reconcile, and a red dot with a count of disagreements when they do not. There is one check array on the page and the shell appends to it rather than creating its own. What is checked:

  • Every ward: ready + held + blocked + occupied equals beds.
  • Every ward: sexMix.Female + sexMix.Male equals occupied.
  • Every ward: locked beds do not exceed total beds.
  • No duplicate movement identifier, and every origin department exists.
  • No movement carries more than three parallel referrals.
  • Every unit a movement points at, and every unit that declined it, exists.
  • Each ward's contention count agrees with the movement list that produced it.
  • The strongest one: a movement may only be pointed at a ward the system would have let it be pointed at. Never one with an absolute gate unmet, and never one needing a recorded reason without that reason on the register.

That last check is what stops the invented dataset from quietly contradicting the rules the screen teaches. Anything built on this standard should keep it.
