    What the blocker is

    One blocker per person, the worst thing holding them up, so these add up to the number of people waiting and never to more. Awaiting a bed is not an ED blocker. The department is the one suffering that, not the one holding it up, so it sits under Wards or Other, and ED counts only what the department itself must finish. Medical clearance is not in this list at all. It is a yes or no that runs alongside the bed rather than a stage of it, so it is marked on the person instead.

    Escalations

    An escalation records that somebody was told. It does not move anybody, and it does not change who owns the blocker.

    Resolved today

    People who were on this screen earlier today and are not now. They are not among the people waiting, and this screen never counts them there: a closed movement lives on the Movement screen, which keeps closed movements on its stage board. Cleared at midnight.

    What is invented and what is real

    Every figure here is invented: the people waiting, their waits, the blocker each is stuck on and how long for, the legal forms and their clocks, the wards asked and their reasons, the escalations, the people resolved today, and the medical clearance state. Nobody here is a real person and nothing on this screen is a clinical record. Every count, tile, group and sentence is derived from that data on each load rather than typed.

    What is real: the emergency departments, the hospital sites, the ward names and the health services, from the repository's own tables. The five owners, the blocker causes, their ranking and the three the screen calls severe are the source page's own and are unchanged.

    Two populations, kept apart: the people waiting are open movements only, one blocker each, counted once. The people resolved today are closed movements, listed in their own register and never added to the people waiting. The Movement screen keeps closed movements on its stage board; this screen does not, and search here refuses them.

    Reconciled to Command: the same day, Saturday 15 August, 10:42, day shift, handover at 14:00, and the same network of departments, wards and sites drawn. The rail's Capacity, Referrals, Handover and Governance figures are the Command third edition's, derived here from the same data. The rail's Command count is rather than fourteen, because this screen's people are the source page's own and Command's fourteen movements carry no blocker, no owner and no stuck clock. The rail marks Command because Delays has no rail item of its own. Both are decisions for the owner.

    What the page says when a list is empty: with no escalation open the register says so. With nobody on a blocker the blocker is still listed, with none in place of a count, so a group that vanishes can never read as nothing being wrong in that category. With nobody selected the right hand panel asks you to choose somebody.
