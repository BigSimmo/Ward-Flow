I sent a chat a brief containing **a false claim** ("its reachability line has moved and the census
says so") **and, in the same brief, the instruction to re-measure rather than work from a list.**

**The recipient re-measured, found the claim false, and the error cost nothing.** Their words:

> ⚠️ **"You supplied both the assertion and the antidote, and the antidote won."**
> **"The brief that carries a fact should carry the instruction to re-derive it."**

**Why:** a relayed fact decays and widens — three of my claims did so in one day, always toward the
more definite version, always because the source was reliable enough that I stopped auditing. **You
cannot reliably stop yourself widening. You can reliably attach the instruction that catches it.**

**How to apply:**

- When a brief states a measured fact the recipient will act on, **say how it was measured and tell
  them to re-derive it**. Not as ceremony — as the thing that makes the brief safe to be wrong.
- Prefer _"measure X before you touch it"_ over _"X is true"_, even when you believe X. **The
  instruction survives your error; the fact does not.**
- This is the general form of several narrower rules: quote the question with the ruling
  ([[the-question-belongs-to-the-answer]]), name the baseline with the count
  ([[a-baseline-from-the-subject-vouches-for-it]]), date a claim about what exists
  ([[no-longer-compresses-to-never]]).

Companion from the same exchange: **a notification is not a check.** Asked to establish whether
another chat still held some files _before_ touching them, a session instead told them _afterwards_
— and said so plainly rather than reporting it as handled. ⚠️ **A check could have stopped it; a
notification cannot.** Do not let the second be recorded as the first.

## 🔴 A SUBAGENT DOES NOT INHERIT YOUR MEMORY. The brief is its entire world. 2026-09-06.

**The clearest instance is mine, and it cost three agents' work.** I hold
[[v2-tokens-beat-globals]], written 2026-09-03, saying _resolve every colour from
`ckb-v2-tokens.css` first_. I then wrote a token-mapping brief that pointed three agents at a
**path that does not exist** and told them a type scale was absent that 45 of 51 stylesheets were
already using. **The memory was in the room for me and not for them** — and I got it wrong myself
in the same breath, so it was not even protecting the author.

A peer hit the same shape from the other side the same evening: they hold a note saying attribution
needs ancestry plus reflog, never `%an`. **Their subagent misattributed a commit to another session
anyway, because the note was never in its brief.** It named a real session in a report one relay
from the chat that folds.

**Three failure modes for a written rule, and this is the third:**

1. a note that does not fire (you did not open it);
2. a note that fires where it does not apply;
3. **a note that cannot reach the party who needs it.**

⚠️ **Only the third is invisible from inside.** The rule was followed, the note was current, and the
agent had no way to know it existed.

**How to apply:** before dispatching, ask _which memories does this task depend on_ and **copy them
into the brief as text** — not as a pointer, which the agent cannot follow. Keep a reusable
preamble for a recurring area rather than re-deriving it each time. **And prefer removing the need:
the peer's better fix was not "put the attribution rule in the brief" but "do not commit an agent's
work mid-flight, and the question never arises."**

⚠️ **A wrong attribution deserves more weight than a wrong figure: it names a real session, so it
travels further and lands harder. A wrong number gets re-measured; a wrong name gets repeated.**

### 🔴 A fourth mode, worse than the third: the rule fails to protect its own author

Named by a peer, 2026-09-06, about my own case. Their subagent lacked the note (mode 3). **I had the
note, was actively working the exact question it answers, and wrote its contradiction into a brief
in the same breath.**

⚠️ **Nothing about that felt like ignoring a rule. It felt like doing the work** — which is why it
is the mode that most resembles ordinary competence from the inside, and why "be more careful"
cannot address it. **The rule was open in front of me and did not fire, because firing depends on
recognising that this is the moment, and misrecognising the moment is the failure itself.**

**The only fixes that work are structural: paste the rule into the artefact where the work happens
(the brief, the comment, the test), or remove the need for the rule** — the peer's better fix was
not "carry the attribution rule" but "do not commit an agent's work mid-flight, and the question
never arises."

**And a brief that only FORBIDS leaves the reader with the same question and no tool.** Their agent
reached for a name in the session traffic because "`%an` is unreliable" would have told it what not
to do and nothing else. **Every prohibition in a brief carries its replacement in the same line.**
