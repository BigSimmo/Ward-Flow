What the colours and the lines mean

Down the middle

The route when nothing goes wrong, in order, from a referral being raised to a bed becoming free again. Read it top to bottom.

Off to the left

Wherever the journey stalls or reverses — in grey, and most of these go back into the main route — or ends, in teal, which nothing follows.

Off to the right

Wherever it carries on somewhere other than expected — a different destination, a bed kept while the patient is elsewhere, a step skipped.

What the line ends mean

An arrowhead is where the route goes next. A bar is a dead end — nothing follows it, and it is drawn in its own colour. A line with a head at both ends goes there and comes back.

Off to the far right

Things recorded alongside the journey without moving it — a legal status changing, an examination, a form arriving. Joined by a dotted line to the point they usually happen.

What the outlines mean

A heavy outline is a point of no return. A red one is something wrong that nothing prevents. A broken one is a stretch the system cannot record at all.
