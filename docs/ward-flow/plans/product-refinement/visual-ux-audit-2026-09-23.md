### 7. Inconsistent hierarchy and navigation make the product feel assembled from separate designs

**Aesthetic judgment and usability, not a functional failure by itself.** The recurring mix of tiny uppercase mono labels, pills, coloured status chips, inset cards and broad shadows creates many local focal points. Similar actions appear as links, solid buttons, outline buttons or full clickable cards. Community reintroduces search/Activity/Tasks/Tools inside the page. Ward offers multiple overlapping routes to its bed view. Patient versus Movement versus Referral identity/context is not consistently explained. Statistics' navigation back to choosers adds steps to compare another unit.

**Recommended direction:** a small vocabulary of heading, row, field, status and action treatments; one obvious task entry; consistent names and destinations; contextual drill-down near the number or record. Keep service colour distinct from warning/confirmation and selected-state colour. **Scope: medium, incremental through the families below.** This is a proposed design direction, not approval of a replacement design system.

## What should be retained

- The cool surfaces and Geist typography are a sound identity. Brass works best for restrained emphasis, not a badge on every detail. Clear slate selection, service labels and textual status alongside colour are worth preserving.
- Capacity's matrix and textual readiness distinctions support comparison. Statistics Compare uses aligned rows effectively. Discharges separates planned/confirmed/blocked work and opens contextual details. These need their own task-appropriate layouts.
- Search Hub's keyboard preview and search recovery are useful; Wards and Patient Search have explicit no-match/reset states. Community Index warns about similarly named entries without silently merging them.
- Command's phone selection sheet is a successful response to limited space. The shared Tasks drawer retained focus during sampled Tab navigation and returned focus to its trigger on Escape. A representative forced-colours check kept text and controls discernible.
- Honest absence is better than a fabricated metric: specialist statistics and governed Patient explain unavailable information. Retain the meaning while reducing repeated prose in the primary work area.

## Recommended first improvement batch — proposed, not authorised implementation

Start with a **small task-access and readability batch**: shared phone shell, ED overflow/name contrast, Referral Board's fitted detail/close control, and Ward/Ward Answer's clipped count/action groups. These are visible, reproducible problems whose correction does not require a broad Command redesign. Review the shared shell on Command, a populated referral and a ward; include phone, desktop, light/dark, long names and selected records. Preserve the diagram/grid and current colour identity while judging the actual result.

Presentation-integrity findings in item 1 should be triaged at the same time as a separate governed work item, before treating any polished screen as trustworthy. They must not be quietly folded into styling or used to invent new clinical behaviour. Next, review one worklist family and one specialist screen, then reuse only the patterns that succeed. No all-screen template replacement or big-bang redesign.

## Flexible design guide for owner review

| Principle               | Proposed application                                                                                                     | Space for specialised screens                                                                             |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Task first              | Page identity, relevant state, next useful action, then the primary work area.                                           | Command can prioritise a queue plus network; a form starts with fields; a report starts with comparisons. |
| Density by purpose      | Compact aligned rows for scanning; room around editing and confirmation; content-sized empty panels.                     | Bed tiles, timelines and diagrams remain distinct where spatial relationships help.                       |
| A small hierarchy       | Page title, section title, record identity, facts, supporting explanation; readable body text before micro-labels.       | Do not enlarge all metrics or force every summary into columns.                                           |
| Surfaces have jobs      | Use a border or surface to separate interactive/grouped regions. Avoid multiple nested containers saying the same thing. | Retain diagram grids or stronger boundaries when they aid orientation; assess visually.                   |
| Stable colour roles     | Platinum/slate foundations, brass emphasis, service association, semantic warning/danger/success, explicit selection.    | Text carries status; service colour does not imply acuity or permission.                                  |
| Honest state language   | Recorded, missing, not assessed, example and completed are distinct. Identify the scope of counts and filters.           | Clinical terminology/rules remain governed by owner decisions, not visual preference.                     |
| Progressive context     | Keep a short limitation beside the relevant value; disclose detailed methodology.                                        | Governance/reporting may need more explanation than operational queues.                                   |
| Phone is task-specific  | Compact global shell; fitted drawers; identity and action visible; intentional table scrolling where necessary.          | Do not simply stack every desktop pane. Complex network views may need a focused drill-down.              |
| Predictable interaction | Clear selected/focus/disabled/error/loading states, reachable close/back, consistent controls for the same action.       | A sheet for quick contextual work; a page for a sustained task. No universal modal requirement.           |

Two preferences would materially affect the next design review, but do not block this audit: **(1)** should phone use prioritise quick patient/task actions or a full operational overview? My recommendation is quick actions with the overview available deliberately. **(2)** on desktop Command, is the network picture or the selected patient's placement work the primary working surface? My recommendation is to preserve both, then allocate space according to the chosen mode rather than making every pane equally prominent.
