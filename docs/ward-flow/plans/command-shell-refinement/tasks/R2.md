## Findings

### P1 — the Officer route is presented as a coordinator and can never receive its addressed notices

`wardChromeRole()` only returns `coordinator`, `ward`, or `ed`, defaulting every unrecognised route to coordinator (`ward-chrome-role.ts:20-35`). The transport-officer route is `/mockups/ward-flow/transport/officer`, so the new rail role card renders “Viewing as Bed coordinator” (`shell/ward-rail.tsx:258-263,458-467`). Activity then requires an exact role/place match (`shell/ward-bar.tsx:274,339-344`), while `CANCEL_TRANSPORT` deliberately creates an officer notice addressed to `{ role: "officer" }` (`ward-flow-reducer.ts:4150-4157`). No route can therefore display that notice, and the Officer screen carries the wrong role identity.

Trigger: cancel booked transport, navigate to Officer, and open Activity. Expected: the rail identifies the transport officer and the addressed cancellation notice is present at or after its raised time. Actual: the rail identifies a bed coordinator and the notice is filtered out.

Smallest repair/proof: extend the route-derived chrome role to cover Officer (including its label/icon and compatible `placeId` behavior), then render an officer-addressed notice on the Officer route and prove the same notice remains absent on Command, ward, and ED routes.

### P1 — Tasks records coordinator actions while the rail says the user is a ward or ED role

The bar already obtains the route-derived role (`shell/ward-bar.tsx:274`) but passes the literal `role="coordinator"` to the globally mounted `WardTasksDrawer` on every route (`shell/ward-bar.tsx:841-850`). The drawer puts that prop directly into acknowledgement/completion events, and the reducer records its human-readable role in the append-only history. Consequently, opening Tasks on a ward or ED route and choosing Acknowledge records “Bed coordinator” even while the adjacent rail says “Nurse in charge” or “ED clinician”. The reducer's own permission contract currently allows these events only for coordinators, so silently substituting coordinator is also the only reason the action succeeds.

Trigger: visit a ward or ED route, open Tasks, and acknowledge any outstanding row. Expected: either the drawer is explicitly coordinator-only and unavailable outside that context, or the acting route role is passed and authorised by an owner-approved permission change. Actual: the action succeeds under an identity different from the one the shell presents.

Smallest repair/proof: do not impersonate coordinator. Choose the product boundary explicitly—limit actionable Tasks to coordinator routes, or authorise and pass the route role—then assert that the displayed role, dispatched role, and recorded `by` value agree.

### P2 — the closed desktop rail exposes three unnamed keyboard links

Settings, Sign-in demonstration, and Exit to developer hub rely on visible child text for their names and give the links no `aria-label` or `title` (`shell/ward-rail.tsx:474-486`). In the closed desktop rail, CSS sets each `.utilityLink > span` to `display: none` (`shell/ward-rail.module.css:463-467`), while all three icons are `aria-hidden`. The links stay in the Tab order but have no accessible name, so a keyboard/screen-reader user encounters three indistinguishable “link” controls.

Smallest repair/proof: retain an accessible name in the compact shape (for example, an `aria-label` sourced from the same visible label) and assert all three closed-rail links have distinct accessible names while remaining keyboard reachable.

### P2 — Command duplicates Movements' counter and creates false corroboration

`renderEntry()` aliases the Command badge directly to `counts.movements` (`shell/ward-rail.tsx:354-361`), while the Movements entry naturally reads that same count. This produces two neighbouring destinations with the identical “still open” figure. The shared count contract explicitly forbids duplicate neighbouring meanings because agreement looks like corroboration rather than one number repeated (`ward-nav-counts.ts:19-24`).

Smallest repair/proof: give Command a distinct, already-derived measure with its own noun, or omit its badge. Assert that Command and Movements do not publish the same count object/meaning.
