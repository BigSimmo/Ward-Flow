Why this is empty

Nothing is missing from the record. Nothing has been drawn. Royal Perth’s wait list, ward-acceptance detail and centrepiece chart were built out in full as the worked example. Drawing the other seven the same way would mean inventing seven more named individuals and seven more distributions, and a prototype that invents that much detail is one somebody will eventually read as a measurement.

This department’s headline figures are not missing — waiting now, median wait, longest wait, how many have passed 24 and 48 hours, how many left before being seen and how many were accepted by a ward in the last 7 days are all in the comparison table below, alongside every other department.

A department is not a ward

This screen only ever describes people currently standing in a department: how many, how long, where they went next, and which wards did or did not accept them. It never shows a bed count, an occupancy figure or a length of stay for the department itself — those belong to the ward screens, and an emergency department has none of its own.

How “three legs” was drawn from three named points

The brief names three points — referral raised, a ward accepts, the patient physically leaves — and asks for time in three legs. Three points make two intervals on their own, so a third boundary was needed and was taken from the domain’s own seven-stage movement model: the ward accepting a referral and the ward pulling a bed and being ready for handover are two different stages, so the middle leg here runs from acceptance to bed-pulled-and-handover-ready, and the final leg from there to physical departure — which lines up with transport being bookable only once the bed is pulled (TR-D4) and being booked by the sending team, not the receiving ward (TR-D1). This is a decision the brief itself did not spell out; it is recorded here rather than silently chosen.

What a 0 means, and what a dash means

A 0 in a count or a band is a genuine measured answer — nobody in that band, nobody over 24 hours. A dash, or the words “not drawn in this prototype,” mean no figure was produced at all. The two must never be read as the same thing.

A zero anywhere on this page is a real, measured zero unless a caption beside it says the figure could not be taken. It is never used to mean “not tracked.”
