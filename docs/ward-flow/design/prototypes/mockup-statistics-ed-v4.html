How the combined median is worked out

The combined median is not an average of the eight medians — it is the median of all 71 people taken together.

Why the other six departments are not drawn as their own charts

Royal Perth was built out in full as the worked example; the other six departments’ headline figures are all in the comparison table above, so nothing is missing, only not drawn twice.

What is real, taken from the repository

  • The eight department names and codes are read from ward-sites.ts, shared with the front-doors mockup.
  • The visual language above the marker is statistics-language-v4.css, copied unedited.
  • The seven-stage movement model and transport rules (TR-D1, TR-D4) behind these figures are taken from statistics-v2-brief.md, unchanged.

What is invented

  • Every count, wait time, distribution, ward acceptance, decline reason and disposition figure on this page.
  • The “as at 11:42” and “last refreshed 06:05” timestamps.

A department is not a ward

This screen describes only people standing in a department, never a bed count or occupancy — those belong to the ward screens.

How “three legs” was drawn from three named points

The third boundary (bed-pulled-and-handover-ready) follows the seven-stage model and the TR-D4/TR-D1 transport rules.

What a 0 means, and what a dash means

A 0 is a genuine measured answer, never “not tracked.” A dash or “not drawn” means no figure was produced — the two are never the same.
