What is real, taken from the repository

  • EMHS’s 4 hospitals and 7 wards, and their exact bed counts. Every hospital name, ward name, cohort and bed count in “This service’s own capacity” is read from ward-sites.ts, the same file the overview screen sources its network totals from. 105 total beds is the arithmetic sum of EMHS’s 7 real ward counts.
  • Every ward’s Ready figure, including the 9 EMHS total, is min(allocatable, empty) computed from that same file’s per-ward allocatable and empty fields — the same derivation the app itself uses, not a figure invented for this page.
  • The four health services and which hospitals sit in each, including SMHS’s, NMHS’s and WACHS’s hospital and bed counts in the switcher, are read from the same file’s service field. “Hospitals” counts only sites carrying at least one inpatient ward, the same convention the network-wide “15 hospitals” figure already uses — Joondalup Health Campus and Peel Health Campus run emergency departments within NMHS and SMHS but hold no inpatient beds of their own, so neither is counted among their service’s hospitals.
  • EMHS’s 3 emergency departments — Royal Perth, Armadale and St John of God Midland — are also read from ward-sites.ts. Bentley Health Service runs no emergency department of its own, which is why it does not appear in either ED line on this page.
  • The three community team names — Midland, Bentley and Inner City — are the exact grouping ward-catchment.ts’s own Royal Perth Bentley Group document uses for this catchment area, and Inner City’s 16 suburbs are counted directly from that file. Midland’s and Bentley’s caseload, referral and discharge figures are reused from the sibling overview screen’s own invented per-team numbers, for one consistent synthetic day.

What is invented

  • Every net-flow figure: the 14 patients sent out and their 8/6/0 split by destination service, the 5 taken in and their 1/0/4 split by origin service, the resulting net export of 9, and the destination-hospital distances (~19 km, ~8 km) in “Where its exported patients went.”
  • Every referral-demand figure in “This service’s own demand”: the 52 raised, 30 placed within EMHS, 3 declined and 5 still open. The 14 sent to another service is the same invented figure as net flow’s 14 sent, not a second, independently invented number.
  • The 31-hour longest wait at Royal Perth Hospital ED, and the underlying per-department wait figures for Royal Perth, Armadale and St John of God Midland, are reused from the sibling overview screen’s own invented emergency-department figures, for the same consistent synthetic day, rather than invented again here.
  • Inner City’s caseload, referral and discharge figures (42 caseload, 3 new referrals, 2 discharged) are invented fresh for this screen, because the overview screen did not show that team among its eight shown of seventeen.
  • The “confirmed 14:20 by 4 of 4 EMHS hospitals” liveness marker and the switcher’s “Net export” chip.

What this screen deliberately does not do

  • It does not compute a combined median wait across EMHS’s three emergency departments. A true median needs the individual wait times behind the three departments’ own figures, not an average of each department’s own median — the same caution the overview screen states about its own network-wide median. This screen leans on the longest wait instead, deliberately: the brief for this page asks for the wait named as one person’s, not a distribution statistic that hides the one that matters.
  • It does not show SMHS’s, NMHS’s or WACHS’s own worked figures. The switcher exists so a reader can see the other three services exist and open theirs; the numbers behind each one belong to that service’s own screen, not to a guess made here.
  • It does not let a coordinator place a patient from this screen. Every figure here is read-only in the same way the overview screen is; a placement action always goes back to the named ward for a phone call.
  • A zero anywhere on this page is a real, measured zero unless a caption beside it says the figure could not be taken. It is never used to mean “not tracked.”
