What is real, taken from the repository

  • 23 units across 15 hospitals — 16 Adult, 6 Older adult, 1 Youth — and which service each belongs to. Read from ward-sites.ts: EMHS (East Metro), SMHS (South Metro), NMHS (North Metro) and WACHS between them hold 21 of the 23 units; St John of God Subiaco's two Adult units are contracted private capacity, not one of the four state health services, which is why they get their own row rather than being folded into one of the four.
  • North Metro's Older Adult unit (Graylands) is genuinely stale in the source model — its last confirmation sits more than three hours past its own staleness window, against the same synthetic “now” (10:42) every other confirmation time on this page is set against. That fact was reused here rather than invented, which is why its Ready count reads as unknown rather than a number.
  • The seven movement stages, in order, and the ruled distinction between “no bed available” and “not a match for any bed”, are the domain model this page is built to, not invented for it.
  • The shared visual language — every colour, spacing value, panel, table, chip, ring and chart primitive above the marker in this file — is statistics-language-v3.css, copied unedited, which is itself a mirror of the application's own ckb-v2-tokens.css.

What is invented

  • Every count on this page. The 47 people waiting for a bed and their +5-on-yesterday movement; the seven stage counts and their median hold times (8, 5, 21, 6, 4, 3 and 2 people; 1h, 2h, 14h, 3h, 1.5h, 45 min and 20 min); WF-013's 31-hour wait at Royal Perth Hospital ED; every Ready-bed count in the service-and-cohort table other than the one stale read; today's and yesterday's referral, acceptance and arrival figures; and every out-of-area placement and rate in the equity table.
  • Every sparkline's shape — the hourly readings behind the waiting-now trend and the yesterday-to-today points in the flow band are illustrative movement in a plausible direction, not read from any recorded series.
  • The “confirmed 10:42 by 14 of 15 hospitals” liveness marker at the top of the page.

What this screen deliberately does not do

  • It has no switcher, and no action that changes a record. This is the top of the hierarchy every ward, department and community-team switcher routes out of; a built version would link each figure onward to the service, ward or team it belongs to, rather than acting on it here.
  • It never adds “no bed available” to “not a match for any bed.” The two counts stay side by side on this page, stated as different problems with different fixes, precisely so a reader does not combine them into one number that implies one cause.
  • A zero anywhere on this page is a real, measured zero. A service or cohort with no unit of that kind says so in words rather than showing a zero it never had to measure, and an unconfirmed figure says “unknown” rather than showing its last known number.
  • It draws no bar charts. The second edition's seven-stage view was a horizontal bar per stage; this edition redraws the same seven numbers as one line, because a bar answers “how big is each” and a line answers “which way is this going” — the question this whole screen exists to answer.
