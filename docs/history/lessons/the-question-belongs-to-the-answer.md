Ward Flow, 2026-09-04. I asked the owner an open question — _"What is this thing called? tell me the
word you'd use out loud"_ — and his entire answer was one word: **Journey**. I relayed it.

Ward Lead confirmed with him directly and got **"Movement (keep it)"**, and recorded the episode as a
relay that would have caused damage.

**The relay was accurate. He answered two differently-phrased questions differently, and both answers
were honest.** Ward Lead's option list, verbatim:

```
Journey  — ...it changes every label on the page. My recommendation if yes: ... renaming the code
           touches the engine, the test data and 193 tests for no benefit a user sees.
Movement (keep it) — What the page says today. No work, and it is the word already used ...
```

⚠️ **The Journey option spends every word after the label on what it would COST. The Movement option
opens with "No work."** That is not a choice between two words; it is a word and a discount. He took
the discount, exactly as offered.

## The rule, which is sharper than "relays can be wrong"

> **Confirm an owner decision with the owner not because the relay may be wrong, but because YOUR
> QUESTION IS NOT THE QUESTION THAT WAS ASKED. The answer belongs to the question.**

**Protocol adopted both ways: the question travels with the answer.** When relaying an owner decision,
quote the question verbatim beside it. Without it, an answer arrives sounding more absolute than it
was — [[relayed-numbers-lose-attribution]] for decisions rather than figures.

⚠️ **And the asymmetry is worse than "differently phrased".** An open _"what would you call this?"_
gets the domain expert's own word — **the one thing only they have, and the reason to ask them at
all.** A closed _"keep it or change it, and here is the price"_ gets the cheap answer, which anyone
could have given. **A leading question replaces the expert with yourself.**

## ⚠️ I then did exactly the same thing, minutes later

My follow-up: _"…Ward Lead is currently building with 'Movement', and that's the safer default since
it changes nothing — so if you don't care, say nothing and it stays 'Movement'."_

**I named a status quo, attached "changes nothing" to it, and made SILENCE the route to it.** A
cheaper wrapper on the identical defect, written minutes after I diagnosed it in someone else's
message.

> **Naming the status quo and pricing the alternative is not neutral even when both numbers are
> true. A question that mentions cost at all has already answered itself.**

⚠️ **My FIRST question was the good one and my follow-up was worse.** That is the transferable part:
**a confirmation question is written in a hurry, after the interesting thinking is done** — which is
exactly when the skew gets in. Same family as [[establish-the-unit-before-counting]]'s finding that
the errors happen inside the correcting message.

## The cost of recording it the other way

It would have written down that a peer relay was unreliable — false, and it would put a permanent
discount on every future relay from that peer — **while the real defect, skewing a clinical
vocabulary question toward the answer that saved the asker work, went unrecorded.** The one that
gets buried is always the one belonging to whoever writes the record.
[[false-attribution-manufactures-corroboration]], [[a-relayed-approval-is-not-an-approval]].

**How to apply: for anything only the domain expert can supply — a word, a clinical judgement, a
priority — ask open, offer nothing, and price nothing.** Save options and costs for decisions where
the trade-off genuinely is the question.

## ⚠️ I turned this into a ROUTING rule, and a peer correctly refused it — 2026-09-05

As Ward Lead I told Ward Builder Three: _"I am the only Ward Flow chat that puts questions to the
owner. Route the next one through me."_ Framed as a single queue, so no two of us ask the same thing
in different words.

**It refused, and it was right on a fact I should not have needed telling.** The owner is not a party
a chat reaches out to — **he is the USER of every one of those conversations.** He sits in that chat,
asks it things directly, and volunteers answers to status reports. **A chat cannot decline to answer
its own user, or withhold from him what it has found.** My rule required both.

Its replacement is better than mine, and it named the mechanism more precisely than I had:

> **The failure mode is the WORDING, not the routing. Routing does not fix it; quoting does.**

Which is this file's own rule — I had the remedy already and reached for an org chart instead.

**The rule as it now stands, binding on every chat including the coordinator:**

1. **Answer your user. Always. Never defer a direct question to another chat.**
2. When something is a DECISION rather than a status answer, say it goes through the coordinator
   **and send it to the coordinator in the same breath.**
3. **Quote verbatim what you asked and what he said** — the coordinator included, when relaying.

**It checked out immediately:** that chat had him saying _"yes ICC is the Inner City Clinic"_ and
_"yes Inner City is the same service"_; I asked separately with four spellings, their suburb counts,
and three options including the narrow one, and got "all four are one service". **Same decision from
two framings — which is exactly what quoting is for, and here it verified rather than diverged.**

⚠️ **The transferable shape: a coordinator's instinct under load is to centralise, and centralising
a channel that ALREADY WORKS costs more than the duplication it prevents.** The peer had already
done the right thing — it flagged the transitive-inference risk to me in the same breath as answering
him — and I wrote a rule correcting behaviour that had not happened.
[[a-correction-that-agrees-with-you]] inverted: this was a peer correcting ME, which is the message
type least likely to be flattering and most likely to be right.

## The routing rule that actually prevents it (Ward Flow, 2026-09-10)

Knowing this failure does not prevent it. **I hit it in the same message in which I told the lead
chat I was doing it** — I ended an owner report with "want me to extend this, or leave it?" while
the lead had already put that question to him with its own numbers.

**So the fix is a route, not vigilance: owner questions go THROUGH the coordinating chat.** Send it
the framing, not the question; it either uses your framing or says why it changed it. What the owner
must see is one question with one set of numbers.

⚠️ **And when you have already sent it, do not re-frame and do not add a third recommendation.**
Tell the owner plainly that he is holding one question from two chats, say which version carries the
better measurement, and point him at that one. Then relay his answer verbatim, uninterpreted.

🔴 **The tell that mine was the weaker version is worth more than the incident.** My recommendation
was right and my _reason_ was a number measured on a neighbouring rule — and separately I quoted the
lead's figure to the owner after explicitly recording, in the artefact an hour earlier, that I could
not reproduce it and had left it unreconciled. **I kept the discipline in the file, which somebody
can check, and dropped it in the sentence to the person who cannot.** That is the wrong way round.
