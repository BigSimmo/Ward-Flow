**Ward Flow, 2026-09-12.** Two owner rulings landed within minutes of each other:

- **O-16.6** — the drawing's **five** community decline reasons become their own list.
- **O-17.11** — a referrer withdrawing becomes a **field**, modelled separately, _because a withdrawal
  is a different act from a decline_.

**The fifth of the five is _"Withdrawn by the referrer before assessment"_.** Build it as ruled and a
withdrawal goes in the decline column — the exact thing the other ruling exists to prevent.

**Why this is not any of the usual traps.** Every catalogued shape so far is ONE claim going wrong:
stale, too wide, unfalsifiable, green over nothing, superseded. **Here both rulings are current, both
are correct, and neither can be criticised on its own.** The defect is that nothing holds them side
by side — and **a contradiction between two documents is invisible from inside either one.**

🔴 **AND THE AUTHOR OF THE EVIDENCE IS THE WORST-PLACED PERSON TO SEE IT.** I supplied the
zero-overlap measurement that produced O-16.6 _and_ the sizing that produced O-17.11. **Having
written both halves is what made each feel settled**, and I nearly built straight past it. The
decision felt like implementation because I already knew both answers.

**How to apply:**

1. **When two rulings arrive close together, enumerate the MEMBERS each one names and intersect
   them.** The collision is never in the summary sentences — it is in one list item. Reading both
   rulings carefully is not enough; you have to lay the items against each other.
2. **Hand it back rather than picking the coherent one.** Choosing option A because it is obviously
   better is scope narrowed by an implementer — the thing you would flag if a peer did it to you.
   **State the lean, name the options, build none of them.** See
   [[a-delegation-is-not-a-word-to-someone-else]].
3. **Say which parts you supplied.** "I measured both halves" is the sentence that tells the
   coordinator why nobody caught it, and it is the part that stops the same pair recurring.
4. **The cheap prophylactic: when a ruling approves a COUNT of things ("the five"), list them.** The
   number is what gets ruled on; the members are where the conflict lives.
