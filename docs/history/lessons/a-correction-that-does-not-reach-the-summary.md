**2026-09-18.** A peer's ledger section was headed _"Two pre-existing reds on the ward line"_. I
amended the body carefully: one was load-dependent flake that had **never failed an assertion**, the
other was real and already fixed. I wrote a paragraph inside it warning that _"a red nobody can
reproduce sends the next session hunting a bug that is not there"_ — and left that exact impression
standing in the heading above it.

🔴 **A correction that does not reach the summary has not been made.** Headings, tables of contents,
first lines, `description:` frontmatter, commit subject lines, section titles — these are what a
skimming reader takes away, and they are the last thing anyone edits because the work felt finished
when the body was right.

## How to apply

- **After correcting a passage, re-read what sits ABOVE it.** The heading, the lead sentence, the
  row in any index, and the file's own summary if it has one. Ask: does this still assert the thing
  I just withdrew?
- ⚠️ **The longer and better the correction, the more likely this is** — effort spent on the body
  feels like completion. Mine ran to two paragraphs.
- **Same shape as** [[a-partial-withdrawal-reads-as-a-careful-one]] (the surviving half inherits the
  correction's credibility) and [[a-retraction-does-not-travel]]. Related:
  [[no-longer-compresses-to-never]], [[observations-expire]].
